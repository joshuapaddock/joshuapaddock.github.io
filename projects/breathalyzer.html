01 How the sensor works

The MQ-3 has a tin-dioxide sensing element held at a few hundred degrees by a built-in heater. Alcohol vapour lowers the element's resistance. The firmware measures that resistance (Rs) through a voltage divider and compares it to a clean-air baseline (R0). The ratio Rs/R0 maps to concentration through a calibration curve.

05 Limitations

  • Temperature and humidity shift the baseline, so readings need compensation or a fresh R0.
  • The sensor also responds to other alcohols and some VOCs (isopropyl, hand sanitizer).
  • It needs a warm-up period every time it powers on.
  • It is not a certified instrument.
