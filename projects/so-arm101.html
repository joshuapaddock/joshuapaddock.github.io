SO-ARM101 leader–follower arm

An open-source, 3D-printed arm pair that I sourced part by part and printed myself. I move the leader arm by hand, the follower mirrors it, and cameras record each demonstration so a policy can learn to repeat the task on its own.

01 Why build it

The goal is a complete learning loop: teleoperate a pick-and-place of a coloured object, record those demonstrations, and train a policy that does the task by itself. I sourced every part myself and printed the frame instead of buying the assembled kit. It cost about half as much, and I had to understand every part of the hardware along the way.

02 Hardware

Each arm has six serial-bus servos: five joints plus the gripper. The follower runs on 12 V servos, and each arm talks to the computer through its own Feetech servo controller board. A wrist camera and an overhead camera give the policy both a close-up and a global view of the scene.

03 Data pipeline

Each demonstration is one episode: synchronized joint positions from the leader and follower plus frames from both cameras. The episodes become the training set for an imitation-learning policy, trained on my RTX 5080 laptop. I'm working through NVIDIA's Isaac Sim-to-Real SO-101 workshop to set up the simulation and training side.

04 Edge inference on the Jetson

Alongside the arm, I deployed and benchmarked quantized LLMs on a Jetson Orin Nano Super to see what fits under edge memory and thermal limits. The longer-term goal is a language interface that calls the arm's controls as tools, with low enough latency for real tasks.
